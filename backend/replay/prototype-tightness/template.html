What does “tight” mean in Qullamägi's own trades?

The detector gates on a trailing 3–7 bar window spanning ≤ TIGHT_MULT × ADR, with TIGHT_MULT = 1.5, and the rubric scores tightness as cluster_k ≥ 5. Both are borrowed defaults. This measures the geometry his actual entries had, at the evaluation session (the last session strictly before entry), so the number can be read off his record.

1 · 1.5 × ADR is not a wall — it is his 64th percentile

The tightest trailing window his entries offered has median 1.31 ADR (p25 1.00, p75 1.73, p90 2.16). The shipped gate at 1.5 sits partway up his own distribution: it keeps of his measured trades and turns away the rest. There is no gap in the histogram at 1.5 — nothing about his record marks that spot.

2 · The “tightest window” diagnostic is just the 3-bar range

Range is monotone in k — a longer window can only contain more high and more low — so the minimum over k ∈ 3..7 is always k = 3. Measured on his record the two distributions are identical to the last decimal, in of trades. cluster_min_range_adr in detection.py therefore reports the 3-bar range under a more general name.

Consequence: K_MIN alone decides whether a name is “tight enough” to enter the funnel; K_MAX only decides the reported cluster_k, which is what the rubric's ×2 tightness dimension then scores.

3 · Tightness pays — and it pays continuously

Median R is −1.00 in every bucket: most of his trades stop out, and the record lives in its tail. So mean R, not median, is the statistic. It falls monotonically as the 3-bar range loosens — with no step at 1.5.

4 · His stop is nowhere near the consolidation low

If “tight” meant “stop under the cluster,” his risk would be about as wide as the cluster: ~1.3 ADR. It is not. His stop is a median ADR wide — × narrower than the base — so he is risking the entry bar, not the base. Both figures are ratios (stop as a fraction of entry, ADR as a fraction of close), so a split cancels out and every one of the trades counts.

This reproduces the committed §6 Finding 1 distribution of references/qullamaggie-replay-findings.md exactly — median 0.345, p25 0.238, p75 0.490, max 2.753 — which is the provenance of STOP_CONVENTION_ADR = 0.345 (issue #127). What is new here is the comparison: his stop against the base width measured on the same nights.

Why the ratio form matters. The trade record's prices are raw while the stored bars are split-adjusted, so any ticker that split after the trade puts the two on different scales. Measuring the stop as a price difference against a bar-derived ADR inherits that error — it gives a median of 0.36 and a max of 30.94 ADR, which is nonsense. The ratio form above avoids it entirely. 5 · What the record says “tight” is

Tight = the last 3 bars spanning about 1.3 ADR or less. That is his median. Not a threshold he respects — a centre of mass he drifts around. Half his entries are looser than 1.3, a quarter looser than 1.73.

Tight is a score, not a gate. Mean R declines smoothly across the whole range (2.02 → 1.35 → 0.84 → 0.35 → −0.36) with no discontinuity anywhere near 1.5. A hard cut at 1.5 throws away of his trades to buy a mean-R improvement the rubric could have got by scoring the same quantity continuously.

“Tight” describes the base; his stop is a separate, much tighter number (median 0.38 ADR ≈ 2.3% of price). Conflating the two would triple his risk per trade.

What this does not settle. Every number here is conditioned on trades he took — there is no counterfactual for the tight bases he passed over, which is what A3's selection contrast covers. Coverage is of the 828-trade record; the missing tickers are absent from the bar store (mostly delisted). And the R figures use the record's own 10-SMA exit, not the app's.
