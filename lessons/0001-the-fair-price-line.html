Lesson 0001 · Stock Trading with VWAP

The Fair Price Line

One skill: look at any intraday chart with VWAP and say, in one sentence, who is in control of the session — and why you're entitled to say it.

What the line is

VWAP is the average price actually paid per share since the session opened, with every trade weighted by its size: 

VWAP = Σ (price × volume) / Σ (volume)     — cumulative from the open

A close at $50.10 on 2 million shares moves it far more than one at $50.40 on 50,000. That weighting is the whole point: a simple moving average tells you where price has been; VWAP tells you what the crowd has paid. It is the session's running fair price (StockCharts: formula and worked example).

Why it isn't just another squiggle

Institutional trading desks are graded against VWAP: a buy order filled below the day's VWAP "beat the benchmark," one filled above it didn't (Madhavan, "VWAP Strategies," 2002). Because millions of institutional shares are scheduled against this yardstick every day (Busseti & Boyd), real order flow is anchored to the line. No moving average has that property — nobody's bonus depends on the 20-EMA.

The control read

Since VWAP is what the average participant paid, price's position against it says who is winning:

  • Price holding above a rising VWAP — the average participant is in profit, dips keep getting bought. Buyers are in control; pullbacks toward the line are where they tend to defend.
  • Price holding below a falling VWAP — the average participant is underwater, rallies get sold into (trapped longs exiting at breakeven). Sellers are in control.
  • Price crossing a flat VWAP repeatedly — no one is in control. This is a real answer, and the discipline to say it is most of the skill. SMB's prop traders use exactly these reads for thesis-building (SMB: Why You NEED VWAP).
The Grimes caveat No free quantitative study shows a statistical edge in VWAP touches by themselves — Adam Grimes tested it and found none, and SMB says outright you cannot trade solely on VWAP. The control read is context: it tells you which trades are allowed, not when to click. Keep this caveat for the whole course.

Check yourself

Answer from memory — one attempt each, feedback is immediate.

Price has held above VWAP all morning and the line slopes upward. The one-sentence read is…

Above a rising VWAP, the average participant since the open is in profit and dips are being bought — the definition of buyers in control.

The average participant since the open is sitting on a loss whenever…

VWAP is the average price paid. Price below it means the average buyer since the open is underwater — the engine behind rallies getting sold in downtrends.

Institutional order flow clusters around VWAP because…

VWAP is the dominant execution benchmark (Madhavan 2002). Fills are judged against it, so large orders are scheduled around it — that grading, not chart magic, anchors the flow.

VWAP is flat and price has crossed it six times since the open. The correct read is…

Repeated crosses of a flat VWAP mean no one owns the session. "No control, no trade" is a complete and correct read — saying it out loud is the skill.

Do this before the next lesson

Once a day, pull up an intraday chart of any liquid stock with session VWAP on it and write one sentence: who is in control, and what is the evidence (side of the line + slope). That's the whole exercise. Next lesson turns the read into an entry: the pullback-to-VWAP with a defined invalidation.
