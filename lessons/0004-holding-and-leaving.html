Lesson 0004 · Swing Trading with VWAP

Holding and Leaving

One skill: manage an open swing without improvising — trail the invalidation as the move earns it, and know which of the two exits you're taking before it happens.

First, from memory

Retrieval before new material — these reach back to earlier lessons deliberately.

Lesson 2: the default swing anchor is…

Enormous volume, total repricing — the AVWAP from the gap day tracks the cohort that repriced the company, and says whether the market accepts the new valuation.

Lesson 3: the entry trigger was…

Multiple tests, then a bar that probes the AVWAP zone and closes back up and away. The touch alone has no proven edge (the Grimes caveat) — defense is the evidence.

The two exits

Every clean swing ends one of two ways. Decide which is live each evening, not in the moment:

  • The thesis break. A daily close below your invalidation level — the cohort visibly stopped defending its breakeven. Exit without negotiation. This is Lesson 3's line, and once price is below the AVWAP the same line flips roles: it now "can act as a resistance level", which is why broken moves stay broken.
  • The strength exit. Price reaches the target, or stretches unusually far above a still-rising AVWAP after a long run. Distance from the line is distance from the crowd's cost basis — the further extended, the more of the move has already happened. Scaling out into that strength sells to eager late buyers instead of fleeing with them later.

Trailing: let higher lows raise the floor

A winning move keeps printing defended higher lows. Each one is new information: a cohort just proved, again, where it will fight. So the invalidation trails up under each defended swing low — Shannon manages exactly this way, trailing stops beneath the higher lows as a trade progresses, with typical holds of three to six days stretching to five or six weeks when the trend keeps answering (CMT Association podcast interview; details via search synthesis — the page blocks automated readers, listen to verify).

Re-anchoring: track the marginal cohort

Weeks into a move, the earnings AVWAP flattens far below price — everyone anchored there is comfortably in profit, and the line stops describing the fight. So plant a fresh anchor at the latest meaningful pivot (the most recent defended swing low): its VWAP is the breakeven of the marginal cohort — the most recent buyers, the first to panic. This is just Lesson 2 applied continuously: a new psychology shift deserves a new line, and StockCharts' multiple-anchors guidance is what makes it mechanical. The old line isn't deleted — it's your disaster floor; the new line is your management tool.

Grade the read, then the exit In review, score three things separately: was the control read right, was the entry a real defense, and did you take the exit your rules named. A trade can lose with all three correct — that's a good trade. The one unforgivable outcome is a fourth category: still holding below a broken line because exiting felt bad.

Check yourself

A daily close below your trailed invalidation means…

The staircase only ever moves up, and a close below it is the thesis break. Negotiating with it converts a defined-risk trade into an undefined one.

As a winning trade ages, you re-anchor because…

The math never breaks — but the fight moves. The latest defended pivot holds the most recent buyers' cost basis, and they're the first to defend or panic. The old line remains as the disaster floor.

Price is stretched far above a rising AVWAP after a long run. That argues for…

Distance above the line is distance from the crowd's cost basis — the extension is the reward being offered. Selling some there is the strength exit; stops never move down.

Do this before the next lesson

Manage the paper trade you planned in Lesson 3 as if live. Each evening, in one line: which exit is closer (thesis break or strength), the current trailed invalidation, and whether tonight's bar changed it. When the trade ends — either exit — write the three-part review from the aside above. That completed cycle, read → anchor → entry → managed exit → graded review, is one full rep of the craft; the next lessons add selectivity (which stocks deserve the rep) and the short side.
