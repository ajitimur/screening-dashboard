Lesson 0005 · Swing Trading with VWAP

Reading the Bands

One skill: replace "it looks extended" with a measured statement — how many standard deviations price sits from the cohort's cost basis, and what that licenses you to do.

Source tier note The free literature on VWAP bands is practitioner guides and indicator documentation (TheVWAP, Trends & Breakouts, GoCharting docs) — a tier below this course's usual sources. The mechanics below are solid; treat every strategy claim as folklore until you've tested it.

What the bands are

VWAP bands are standard-deviation envelopes around the AVWAP, built from the same cumulative volume-weighted data as the line. The running variance is the volume-weighted second moment of price minus the mean squared: 

σ² = Σ (volume × price²) / Σ (volume) − VWAP²      — cumulative from the anchor
bands: VWAP ± 1σ, ± 2σ (sometimes ± 3σ)

Because the weighting is by volume, the interpretation stays in cost-basis language: the AVWAP is the average price the cohort paid, and ±1σ brackets where the bulk of that cohort actually transacted. The bands are not a prediction — they're a map of the cohort's positions.

Why statistical distance beats dollar distance

Two dollars above VWAP is inside 1σ on a volatile name and beyond 3σ on a sleepy one. The band answers the only question that matters for extension: unusual for this stock, this anchor, this regime — or not? That converts three judgment calls from earlier lessons into measurements:

Earlier ruleBand upgrade
"Scale out when stretched far above the line" (Lesson 4)Scale out into strength at or beyond +2σ above a rising AVWAP — unusually far from the crowd's cost basis.
"Price pulling back toward the line" (Lesson 3)A pullback from +2σ to the line is a real reversion to cost basis. A dip that never left +1σ barely pulled back — demand less of it, or skip it.
"Buyers in control" (Lesson 1)Closes hugging the upper band while the line rises = band-walking, trend strength. It is not a short signal.

The three honest warnings

  • 2σ ≠ 95%. Returns are fat-tailed and trending, so the normal-curve intuition fails: in a strong trend price rides outside +2σ for days. Bands locate unusualness; they do not promise reversion.
  • Bands widen as the anchor ages. Variance only accumulates, so an old anchor's bands go slow and wide — one more reason for re-anchoring: fresh anchor, responsive bands.
  • The Grimes caveat, squared. If a VWAP touch has no proven standalone edge, a band touch has less. Bands grade extension for rules you already have — they never generate the trade.

Check yourself

The ±1σ band around an AVWAP brackets…

The variance is volume-weighted, so the bands map the distribution of prices actually paid since the anchor — the cohort's positions, not a prediction.

A pullback that traveled from +2σ back to the AVWAP is…

That's the full trip from "unusually far above the crowd's cost" back to the crowd's cost. It upgrades the pullback's quality — the entry still waits for Lesson 3's defense.

Price closes hugging the +2σ band for several days while the AVWAP rises. That is…

Band-walking. Fat-tailed, trending returns keep price outside 2σ for days in strong moves — shorting the band in a rising trend is fighting the cohort that's winning.

The +2σ band fails to contain ~95% of prices because…

The 95% figure assumes independent, normally distributed observations. Price series are neither — tails are fat and moves trend, so 2σ marks "unusual," never "impossible."

Do this before the next lesson

Add bands to the daily exercise: on your earnings-anchored VWAP, note each evening where price sits in band terms (inside 1σ, between 1σ and 2σ, beyond 2σ) alongside the control read. If your Lesson 3 paper trade is running, restate its status once in band language: how extended is it, and would +2σ change tonight's exit decision? One sentence each.
