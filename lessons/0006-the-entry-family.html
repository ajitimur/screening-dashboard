Lesson 0006 · Swing Trading with VWAP

The Entry Family

This lesson teaches one skill. Given a chart, name which member of the entry family is on the table, if any, using the shared skeleton to decide. The pullback was never the only entry. It was the first because it's the purest.

The skeleton every entry shares

Strip any legitimate AVWAP entry to its parts and the same four appear:

  1. A control read. Who owns the move (Lesson 1).
  2. A cohort level. A price with real breakevens behind it: an AVWAP, a convergence zone, a defended low. Never an envelope (Lesson 5).
  3. Evidence. The market demonstrating the level matters now: defense, or a hold after a cross.
  4. A pre-written invalidation. The daily close that proves the thesis wrong (Lesson 3).

Miss one and it isn't a trade. It's a location, a hunch, or a hope. The Entry Playbook reference holds the whole family as a one-page checklist. This lesson teaches the two members you haven't met.

The reclaim: buying the flip

The pullback buys an established winner's dip. The reclaim buys the moment control changes. Price has been living below the AVWAP, sellers in control, and then crosses back above it and holds. SMB describes exactly this structure: "multiple tests against the VWAP… once I get the confirmation of the crossover and holding, that is an area of interest". TrendSpider's documentation of Shannon's indicator frames the same trade as a breakout above the anchored VWAP.

The mechanics on daily bars: the cross is a daily close above the line. The hold is what you actually pay for. The next dip makes a higher low above the reclaimed line, proving the old sellers' resistance has become the new buyers' support. Enter on that higher low's defense. Invalidate on a daily close back below the line, which means the flip failed.

Know what you're buying. The reclaim catches turns far earlier than the pullback: you're in near the bottom of a control change instead of partway up a trend. It also fails more often, because most first attempts to flip control get rejected. Two reads make an attempt more credible: the losses below the line were aging (higher lows pressing up into it, price spending less and less time far below it), and the reclaiming bar comes on conviction, not drift.

The breakout, with the AVWAP beneath

Here the AVWAP is not the entry level at all. Price consolidates under a visible resistance while holding comfortably above a rising AVWAP, meaning the cohort is positioned underneath, absorbing supply. The trigger is a daily close through the resistance. The line's job is confirmation (the base was accumulation, not distribution), and after entry it's where the Lesson 4 trailing lives. Shannon's framing, that AVWAP helps "to time breakouts and breakdowns", is precisely this supporting role. Its cost: the natural invalidation (back inside the base) sits further from the entry than a pullback's does, so the R is worse. Size smaller.

Choosing among them

The family isn't a menu of equivalent options. The chart's state picks for you:

Chart stateEntry on the table
Rising AVWAP, price returning to itPullback (into a stacked zone: convergence pullback)
Price below the line, sellers' control agingReclaim, after cross and hold
Base under resistance, rising AVWAP beneathBreakout, AVWAP confirming
Falling AVWAP, price rallying into itNothing long. (The short mirror, a future lesson)
Flat AVWAP, repeated crossesNothing. Chop is a complete answer
Keep the caveat Adding entries doesn't add edge. The Grimes caveat applies to every member equally. The claimed edge is always the full skeleton: control, cohort level, evidence, asymmetric R, graded honestly in review. More entry types just means more charts where the skeleton can be found.

Check yourself

Every entry in the family requires…

The four-part skeleton. Miss one and you have a location or a hope, not a trade.

The reclaim entry triggers on…

SMB: "confirmation of the crossover and holding." On daily bars: close above the line, then a higher low defended above it. The cross alone is noise.

Compared with the pullback, the reclaim…

You're buying a control flip near its start instead of an established trend's dip. Earlier in the move, but most first flip attempts get rejected. The invalidation (close back below) is doing heavy work.

In the breakout entry, the AVWAP's job is…

The trigger is the close through resistance. The rising line beneath says the base was accumulation and gives the trail a home after entry. A supporting role, per Shannon's "time breakouts and breakdowns."

Which line has a defending cohort behind it?

Only the AVWAP is somebody's breakeven, the cohort that repriced the stock at earnings. Bands are envelopes, and round numbers are psychology without a specific cohort's cost basis. (This is Learning Record 0002's distinction.)

Do this before the next lesson

Extend the daily exercise from a read to a classification. For your one stock each evening, name which row of the playbook table the chart is in, including "nothing," which will be the answer most days. When your paper trade from Lesson 3 completes, plan the second one as a reclaim if you can find a candidate: same written plan, and note explicitly what the "hold" evidence was. Two entry types paper-traded beats four entry types read about.
